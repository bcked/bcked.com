Methodology

bcked records three things for every tracked token: its price, its supply and its backing. All data is collected by the open-source adapters in bcked-adapters, stored as plain CSV files on its records branch and published through the bcked API.

Terms

  • Backing is what an issuer holds so that a token can be redeemed for a fixed amount of something else. Backing reduces risk but is no guarantee: every token has its own risks.
  • Reserve asset is an asset at the end of a backing chain, like US dollars, Treasury bills, gold or bitcoin.
  • Backing chain is the path from a token through the tokens and reserves behind it, for example a token backed by WBTC, which is backed by bitcoin.
  • Composition is the mix of reserve assets behind a token, as shares of the total or in US dollars.
  • Backing ratio is the value of the backing divided by the market cap, see below.

Backing

Backing is what an issuer holds for a token: cash, government debt, gold, other tokens. It is recorded in units of the underlying asset (US dollars, troy ounces, BTC), not in US dollars, so it can be valued with the price at any time.

  • On-chain backing is read from the contracts that hold it, for example the ETH held by the WETH contract.
  • Reported backing comes from the issuers' attestation reports or reserve pages. Each report date is stored with the time the report states, converted to UTC. Reserve categories are mapped to assets as closely as the report allows. Commercial paper, money market funds, secured loans, equities and similar categories are their own reserve assets, counted at the US dollar value the report states. Reserves whose composition the issuer does not report are shown as "Composition not reported": Tether's "other investments", sums of several kinds of assets in one line, and periods where a report only states the total.
  • Reserves for several chains. Some issuers report one reserve for a token on all chains (Tether, Circle). The token on one chain gets the share of the reserve that matches its share of the circulating supply on that day, based on DefiLlama.

Every backing value is shown with the date it refers to. Reports appear monthly or quarterly, so the backing of some tokens is weeks or months older than their price. The asset page says so when the backing is more than 30 days older than the market cap.

Backing ratio

The backing ratio is the US dollar value of the backing divided by the token's market cap at the same time. A ratio of 100% means the reported reserves are worth exactly the tokens in circulation.

The market cap uses the circulating supply where it is known. Tokens held in the issuer's own treasury are not circulating and not backed, so including them would understate the ratio.

"UNK" means the backing is unknown, not zero. bcked does not rate tokens or issuers, and a ratio is no statement about the safety of a token.

Gaps and corrections

From November 30, 2025 to October 27, 2026 the crawler did not run. Prices, supplies and on-chain backing for that period were filled with daily values from the sources below. WBTC backing was rebuilt from the bitcoin transactions of its custodian addresses, and matches the recorded holdings on both ends of the gap.

Tether published no reserve reports from November 2018 to January 2021. For that time bcked shows the monthly total assets from Tether's own transparency page, as archived by the Wayback Machine, as "Composition not reported". These totals are unaudited.

If a value is wrong, please report it with the correction form.

Sources

  • Prices and stablecoin supply: DefiLlama, DexScreener
  • Ethereum data: Powered by Etherscan.io APIs, Alchemy
  • Bitcoin: BitGo (price), Blockstream (block height)
  • WBTC custody: wbtc.network
  • Reserves: Tether, Circle, Paxos, BlackRock Circle Reserve Fund
  • US dollar supply: Federal Reserve H.6. Treasury securities: TreasuryDirect
  • Gold supply: USGS until 2018, above-ground stock of the World Gold Council from 2019
  • Gold price: Swissquote
  • Backfill of the crawler gap: mempool.space, ultrasound.money, dRPC, currency-api
